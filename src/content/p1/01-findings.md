1. **Tulips will be over by 30 May if left in the ground.** In southern England, even late tulips peak from mid-April to mid-May. Your handwritten plan (pull early, no water, wrap in newspaper, fridge, rehydrate) is exactly right and is not optional. Plan to pull the wedding tulips around 9–16 May at "colour showing" bud stage.

2. **Five plants on your wedding list cannot realistically flower by 30 May** from seed sown now: scabiosa, Didiscus, Achillea, clary sage and Lunaria. They are marked **Not in time.** in [Part 2](#/p2/wedding), each with a fix — buy plants, use foliage, or save for a later event. Two more, the Iceland poppies, are gambles (**Gamble.**): sow them now anyway, but expect 0 to 1 stem per plant on the day. 'Bridal Silk' poppy is borderline (**Borderline.**).

3. **Several crops need warmth your unheated cold frame cannot give,** however many trays it holds. Tomatoes, peppers, onions, basil, Didiscus, eucalyptus, scabiosa (main sowing), rudbeckia, statice, lemon bergamot, lavender and helichrysum need a heated propagator or warm windowsill at home for germination.

4. **Rodents are the number-one threat to your wedding.** Bulbs, corms and sweet pea seed are what mice, voles and squirrels dig up first. [Part 5](#/p5/threats) is a full defence plan, built into the layout.

5. **Your list contains a whole family of brassicas in disguise.** Stocks, Thlaspi, Lunaria and candytuft are cabbage relatives. They share clubroot and flea beetle with your two cabbages, so they are rotated together in [Part 4](#/p4/rotation).

6. **Autumn sowing is the single biggest lever for May flowers.** Hardy annuals sown in October and November flower 4–6 weeks earlier than spring sowings. You are at the late end of the window, so sow this week.

7. **Your cold frame holds about 18 trays, and it is nearly full by 10 March** (17.5 of 18). It runs on a schedule: the [tray ledger](#/p3/cold-frame) shows what occupies it and when, and what to drop first if it overflows.

8. **Back-up plans are built in.** Every wedding flower has a second batch, a later sowing, or a cold-store trick so that no single failure empties the buckets.
