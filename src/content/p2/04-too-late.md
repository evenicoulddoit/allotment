Five plants on the wedding list cannot realistically reach 30 May from seed sown now. Each has a fix. Two more — the Iceland poppies and lemon bergamot — are marked differently, so read their rows carefully.

| Plant | Why not in time | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Achillea 'The Pearl' | Seed-raised plants flower from July in year one. | Buy a 1 L plant in spring, or substitute extra Orlaya/Nigella as the white filler. |
| Clary sage | Biennial (Salvia sclarea): flowers in its second year, 2028. If your packet is the annual Salvia viridis, autumn sowing may flower in June. | Check the packet. Use foliage if neither flowers. |
| Lunaria | Biennial: sown now, flowers spring 2028. | Sow in June 2027 for the following year. |
| Didiscus | Half-hardy annual, no heated bench. | Sow Jan–Feb on a warm windowsill. Treat any May flowers as a bonus. |
| Scabiosa | Flowers from June. Autumn sowing may bring early flowers. | Sow some now in the frost-free end of the frame as a gamble, plus Feb on a windowsill. |
| Iceland poppies (both) | Packet windows (Apr–Jun, May–Jul) assume the packet's own sowing time. Ideal sowing is June–September for flowers the following spring; an end-October sowing gives small plants into winter. | Now a gamble, not a red cross: sow now, plant out 15–31 Mar, expect 0 to 1 stem a plant by 30 May and flowers mainly June onward. |
| Lemon bergamot | Flowers July–September. | Use the lemon-scented foliage on 30 May. |
