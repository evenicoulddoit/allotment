## Layer 3. Put the tastiest plants where you walk

- **A daffodil ring around planters T and R.** Plant daffodils every 15 cm along the border, 10–15 cm deep. Mice, voles and squirrels leave daffodils and alliums alone because they are toxic, so these bulbs act as a living fence. Alliums are the same.
- **Sweet peas (planters S1 and S2) at the north end of the main path,** so the path leads straight to them and damage is noticed in days.
- **Keep beds apart** with short, mown paths so rodents must cross open ground to reach them.

## Layer 4. Monitor and trap

- **Weekly patrol (5 minutes):** look for 2–3 cm tunnel holes (mice and voles), 5–8 cm holes (rats), gnawed stems, pulled-up bulbs and droppings.
- **Snap traps in covered tunnels.** Use a plank or a length of pipe so no birds or hedgehogs can reach them. Put 6 along fence lines and path edges. Bait with peanut butter or chocolate spread. Set at dusk, check every morning.
- **Rats:** tell the site representative. Many allotment sites prohibit poison for good reason, because it harms owls, foxes and hedgehogs. Use traps in locked boxes.
- **Insurance:** keep roughly 15% more tulip and ranunculus plants than you strictly need. The second tulip batch in January is also your reserve.
