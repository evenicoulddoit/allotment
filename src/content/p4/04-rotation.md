## Succession: where space is reused

After the early-pulled tulips and the faded ranunculus clear in June, planter T becomes the home for 'Red Kalibo' cabbage (8 plants, with marigolds), planted from modules 1–15 June, and planter R takes a late sowing of carrots and beetroot (mid-June to mid-July) under insect mesh. Feed with compost first because cabbage is a hungry crop after bulbs.

## Rotation by plant family and bed

**Year 2 (2028) moves:** the brassica block (bed 2) moves to bed 1's ground, and the wedding annuals (bed 1) move to bed 2's ground. Onions and chives (bed 5) swap with carrots and beetroot (bed 6) only if the onion bed has had white rot; otherwise swap onions with tomatoes (bed 8). Summer cutting (bed 7) and tomatoes (bed 8) swap places. Beds 4 and 9 (perennials and herbs) and the planters stay put.

Keep orlaya and Didiscus (carrot family) at least a metre from carrots in every year.

Crops in the same family share pests and diseases, so they travel together and never follow themselves in the same soil.

| Family | On your list | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brassicaceae (cabbage family) | Cabbage ×2, stocks ×3, Thlaspi, Lunaria, candytuft | Keep in one block. Clubroot lives in soil for up to 20 years. Never follow brassicas with brassicas. Use flea-beetle mesh. |
| Amaryllidaceae (onion family) | Onion, chives, allium | Onion white rot persists. Rotate. Use fleece against allium leaf miner in Oct–Nov and Mar–Apr. |
| Apiaceae (carrot family) | Carrots ×2, orlaya, Didiscus | Orlaya and Didiscus are not carrot-fly hosts, but keep them separated from the carrot bed as a precaution. |
| Solanaceae | Tomatoes ×2, peppers ×2 | No potatoes nearby (blight). New bed each year. |
| Asteraceae (daisy family) | Dahlias, rudbeckia, marigold, helichrysum, cornflower, achillea | Slugs and aphids favour these. Mix with aromatic herbs. |
| Ranunculaceae | Ranunculus, larkspur, nigella | Rotate cut-flower beds yearly. |
| Papaveraceae | Poppies ×3 | Self-sow freely. Do not disturb roots. |
| Lamiaceae (mint family) | Herbs, lavender, clary sage, lemon bergamot | Mints spread. Contain them. |
| Rosaceae | Strawberry, geum | Vine weevil risk across both. Inspect pots on arrival. |

## Design rules I used

- Rodent-attractive plants live in the most-visited, most-fenced places: the tulip and ranunculus planters (T, R) sit nearest the entrance, and the sweet pea planters (S1, S2) at the end of the main path, so damage shows quickly. Tulips and sweet peas are the likely targets.
- Daffodils and alliums ring planters T and R. Mice, voles and squirrels avoid them, so they act as a living border.
- Tall to the north, short to the south so nothing shades the cutting beds.
- Aromatic herbs at the edges of vegetable beds to confuse pests. This reduces damage but does not replace netting.
- Marigold and nigella throughout as hoverfly magnets, because hoverfly larvae eat aphids.
- No bare soil. Mulch with compost or leaf mould so weeds do not move in. Do not rely on ground cover to keep slugs out: it can shelter them.

> **Undersowing** means sowing a low, quick-growing cover plant, such as clover, among or beneath a taller crop that is already growing, so the soil is never bare. For the wedding beds a compost mulch does the same job without competing for water.
